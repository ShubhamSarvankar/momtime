# 0061. The ringer, the audio and the overlay route

Date: 2026-10-04
Status: Accepted

Decided by Claude (technical review) in the PR 5 prompt; implemented in `phase-2/delivery`. It builds on ADR 0017 (one alarm at a time) and ADR 0060 (delivery paths). Items marked "judgment" are the implementing session's, for review.

## Decision

**The ringer is a foreground service of type `mediaPlayback`** (`RingerService`), started by the delivery port when a fire takes a ring path. The alarm is `setAlarmClock` (ADR 0017); this is what a fired alarm starts, and it plays the alarm sound until it is stopped. The start happens under the exact alarm exemption: a broadcast from `setAlarmClock` may start a foreground service from the background. It is not exported. It writes nothing to any log or table. Boot never starts it (CLAUDE.md): on Android 15 a boot receiver cannot start a `mediaPlayback` foreground service, and nothing the ringer does is reachable from one.

**Permissions.** `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK` is the type specific permission a `mediaPlayback` service needs from Android 14, declared with `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` (which WorkManager already brings, ADR 0057). Both are normal permissions granted at install. Play asks for a declaration of what a foreground service type is used for: playing the alarm sound of a reminder the user set. `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` (below) is the other addition. Both are in `verifyManifestPermissions`'s allowlist with their reasons.

**A refusal degrades, never crashes.** The platform may refuse the start: `ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException` (an `IllegalStateException`), a `SecurityException`, any other refusal, in the call that starts the service or inside it when it goes foreground. The delivery port catches a refusal from the start, and the service catches one inside itself and tells its host. Either way the ring degrades to the notification path, the same notification is posted (with a full screen intent only while it is effective), the ring session ends so that the next rung tries the ringer again, and the refusal is recorded in the android store, which is non fatal. Nothing on the fire path may throw over it.

**Audio** is `USAGE_ALARM` with sonification content, played on the alarm stream, so it carries through Do Not Disturb without notification policy access (CLAUDE.md), which the app never requests. The notification carries `CATEGORY_ALARM`. Transient audio focus is requested first and the outcome recorded; the sound plays whatever the answer is, because an alarm that does not sound because another app holds focus is worse than two sounds. The sound loops. If the bundled sound cannot be played the system's alarm tone is the fallback, because a silent ring is worse than the wrong sound. `AlarmAudio.attributes()` is one function, and a test pins it.

**The sounds are placeholders.** `ring_primary.wav` is generated by `scripts/sounds/GenerateSounds.java`, run with a JDK and no dependency: 16 bit mono PCM at 22050 Hz, exactly 2.0 seconds, three 880 Hz beeps and a pause, faded in and out over 10 ms so that the loop has no click. It is synthetic and made by that script alone, so it has no author or licence to credit; the script is deterministic, and its output is committed. It is far under the 30 second limit. Shubham chooses the final sounds. The volume ramp and the louder backup sound are PR 5b.

**The overlay route.** With `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` granted, the app may start an activity from the background, which is how the ring screen is opened when a full screen intent is not available. It is used only when the effective full screen intent is false and the permission is granted: on `HEADS_UP` and `AUDIO_ONLY`, never on `RING`, never on a silent path. It is a secondary route to showing the screen and never the ringing mechanism; the ringer is. On Android 15 holding it restricts starting a foreground service from the background, which is not relied on here: the ringer starts from the alarm's broadcast.

**The show intent.** `setAlarmClock`'s show intent is no longer null. Until Phase 3 the app has one activity, so it opens the ring screen, which with nothing ringing says so. Judgment: the prompt said "it opens the app", and the ring screen is the app's only screen now; Phase 3 points it at the app's own.

## Alternatives considered

- **Start the ringer from the Tier 1 broadcast.** Rejected: the exemption is for exact alarms.
- **Play the sound from the receiver without a service.** Rejected: a receiver has seconds, and the process may be killed while it rings.
- **`MediaSession` or a full `Notification.MediaStyle`.** Not needed to play an alarm, and it would invite media controls on an alarm.
- **Request notification policy access to override Do Not Disturb.** Rejected by CLAUDE.md: `USAGE_ALARM` carries through, and the access invites review scrutiny for no benefit.

## Limits

Robolectric shows the service goes foreground with the media playback type, starts a fake sound once and records focus, and that a refusal degrades. It does not show that a device plays the sound, at what volume, over Do Not Disturb, or that a real `startForegroundService` from a real alarm broadcast is allowed on every release (`MANUAL_CHECKS.md` P2-16, P2-17, P2-12 and the existing P2-11).
